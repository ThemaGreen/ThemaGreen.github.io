About

I like to try new things and create.

Most recently that means systems engineering as it applies to autonomous systems in aerospace and aviation — which sounds like a mouthful, but is essentially the cross-section between AI and planes.

I'm a systems thinker and, fingers crossed, a future systems engineer. I was recently accepted into the highly selective Systems Engineering M.S. program at Embry-Riddle Aeronautical University, and I currently work as a human-centered AI Research Scientist at the Institute for Human-Centered AI here in DC.

I'm an unfortunate member of Gen Z — one of the last cohorts to grow up without smartphones, which means I grew up alongside the wild west that was the early internet. They call us the digital nomads. Gifted kid, straight-A student; you know how that goes.

As a kid I always wanted to know the why. Always asking questions, always demanding clarity. I'm sure my parents were sick of me for it, justifiably so. That instinct stayed, and it has shaped every chapter since — it gave me a keen awareness of how technology can both empower and endanger the people using it. Today I channel that into human-centered design and research. Plus, growing up, I thought scientists were pretty cool.
