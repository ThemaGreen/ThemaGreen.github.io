GMU library website redesign

Research led update of a large university library site using usability testing, heuristic reviews, and competitor scans to improve student workflows.

Problem: Low student engagement Process: User testing and heuristic review Outcome: 35 percent higher task success in prototype tests

Context

This project focuses on a major university library site that students rely on for research, course materials, and campus information.

Early observations suggested that students were not discovering key resources efficiently, which pointed to navigation and information architecture issues rather than a lack of content.

Approach

  • Ran usability tests on core student workflows such as finding articles, booking resources, and accessing support content.
  • Completed heuristic evaluations to flag issues in navigation, labeling, and layout that made common tasks harder than necessary.
  • Reviewed competitor and peer library sites to identify patterns that supported smoother student journeys.

Key insights

  • Students frequently took indirect routes to reach common tasks, indicating that key entry points were either mislabeled or buried.
  • Several navigation and content organization issues were consistent across participants, giving the team clear candidates for redesign.
  • Prototype updates that surfaced high‑value actions and clarified labels led to higher task success in testing.

Outcome and reflection

In prototype tests, the redesigned flows achieved the reported 35 percent increase in task success, showing the impact of relatively focused navigation and layout changes.

The work also reinforced how combining usability testing, heuristic review, and competitor scans can quickly reveal where a large content site is working against its users.
